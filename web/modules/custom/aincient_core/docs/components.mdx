Every page you build in the [Pages room](/page-studio) is assembled from a curated
component library. The assistant never writes HTML or CSS — it arranges these named
**sections**, and each one is individually designed and reskins automatically from your
brand. The set is deliberately broad: you can build a marketing site, a company site, or a
news/blog site from it without custom components.

## Sections

Sections stack top to bottom to make a page. You ask for them in plain language ("add a
pricing section", "put testimonials after the features") and the assistant places them.

### Opening and structure

- **`hero`** — the top-of-page opener, the only full-viewport section. Centered or split
  with an image. Open every page with one.
- **`logos`** — a quiet row of partner or customer logos for instant "trusted by"
  credibility.
- **`divider`** — a light separator between sections: a rule, breathing space, or a small
  centered label.

### Telling your story

- **`content`** — a prose block paired with an image (or text-only). Alternate the image
  side as you stack these.
- **`markdown`** — a block of long-form body text you author in Markdown (headings, lists,
  links, bold/italic, blockquotes), rendered with a reading-optimised prose measure. Reach
  for it when you have free-form formatted copy that no structured section models.
- **`features`** — a heading plus a grid of feature cards with icons.
- **`stats`** — a row of headline numbers for social proof.
- **`gallery`** — a set of images, in one of two arrangements:
  - **grid** (the default) — a tiled overview: screenshots, product shots, team photos.
  - **carousel** — one image at a time, shown whole and never cropped. Visitors step
    through with the arrow buttons, the arrow keys, or a swipe. Made for artwork, a
    portfolio piece, or a photo series.

  Turn on **lightbox** and clicking an image opens it full size, with arrows to move
  between images and Esc to close. See [Show artwork one image at a time](#show-artwork-one-image-at-a-time).
- **`image`** — a single standalone image with an optional caption (a figure). Reach for it
  to place one picture on its own — a screenshot, photo, diagram, or chart — when `content`
  (image *with* text) and `gallery` (a *set* of images) don't fit.
- **`banner`** — a short, bold full-width statement band.

### Building trust

- **`testimonials`** — customer quotes, one strong quote or a grid of several.
- **`team`** — the people behind the company, with head-shots, names, and roles.
- **`faq`** — frequently-asked questions in an expand-to-read list (plain-text answers).
- **`accordion`** — an expand-to-read list whose panels hold **rich** content (formatted
  copy, lists, links — and a figure), not just plain text. Each panel can mix Markdown text
  and a single image. Reach for it for layered detail — documentation, how-tos, spec
  breakdowns. Set it to open one panel at a time, or allow several at once.

### Converting

- **`pricing`** — plan tiers side by side, with one tier highlighted.
- **`newsletter`** — an email sign-up band.
- **`cta`** — a closing call-to-action band. Close every page with one.

## Layout

- **`grid`** — a bounded grid of equal **card** tiles. Reach for it only when no named
  section fits — for example a set of resource links or related items. You set the number
  of columns and the tiles reflow.

<Tip>
  You rarely place a `grid` directly — most layouts are better served by a named section
  like `features` or `gallery`. The assistant picks the right one for what you describe.
</Tip>

## Choose which components your site uses

Open **Site → Components** in the console to see every component your site offers and
decide which ones it uses. Only administrators see this room.

- **The centre shows each component, live.** Every component renders from a built-in
  example in your published brand, so you see what you are offering before anyone places
  it. Click one to look at it alone, switch its tone, and check it at phone (375),
  tablet (768) and desktop (1280) widths.
- **The rail on the right is where you decide.** Components are grouped by what they do
  (Openers, Story, Media, Social proof, Conversion, Structure, From your site), and each
  row shows how many pages and blocks use it. Search, or filter to **Unused**, **Off** or
  **Packs**.
- **Expand a row** to read what the component is for, turn off individual variants or
  tones for that component alone, and see the pages and blocks that use it.
- **Nothing changes until you Publish.** Untick freely, then **Publish** to apply it, or
  **Discard** to drop the changes. **Tones everywhere** turns a tone off for
  every component at once; at least one tone always stays on.

### Page types and blocks

The **Applies to** menu at the top of the rail switches what you are deciding for:
**Everywhere** (the whole site), one **page type** such as Landing page, or **Block**
under *Reusable* (the reusable blocks you place on pages). A component turned off everywhere shows as
locked in every page type, with a link back to Everywhere.

For a page type you also choose:

- **New components from packs** — *Allowed automatically* offers a newly installed pack's
  components on this page type straight away; *Off until I allow them* keeps them off
  until you tick them.
- **Opener** — the section every new page of this type starts with, such as a hero.
- **Max per page** — in a component's expanded row, how many of it one page may hold.

A page type with a fixed layout, such as a blog post, has nothing to place, so it shows
a notice instead of components.

### Publishing shows what it affects

When a change touches pages or blocks that already use something you turned off,
**Publish** first lists them, grouped by page, and asks you to **Publish anyway** or
**Cancel**. Nothing on those pages changes; the list is so you know where the
**No longer offered** markers will appear.

### Ask the Components assistant

The chat beside the room can **review your components** — which ones fit what your site
is for, which are unused, and which tones read poorly with your published brand — and
make changes you ask for, everywhere or for one page type ("we never sell online, turn
off pricing", "landing pages should open with a hero"). It never publishes: a change it
makes appears in the rail as unsaved, and you review the affected pages and **Publish**
it yourself, or **Discard** it.

### Components from packs

Components that come from a [component pack](/component-packs) show the pack's name and
sit under **Other**. A pack component that doesn't meet Atelier's rules is listed under
**Can't be used**, with the reason. When a pack replaces one of the built-in components,
the row shows **Replaced by** and the pack's name: select it to see the pack's version,
the original, or both side by side (**Compare**), and use **Use original instead** to
go back to the original on this site. Like every change here, it applies when you
Publish.

### Turning a component off keeps existing sections

A component you turn off can't be added again — the assistant stops offering it and it is
gone from the section picker — but **sections already on your pages stay and keep
rendering**. The same goes for a variant or tone you turn off. In the
[Pages room](/page-studio) those sections show a **No longer offered** marker; you can
still edit them, and **Replace…** swaps one for a component your site still offers, in
the same spot.

## Show artwork one image at a time

A portfolio page usually wants each piece seen whole, one after another, and opened full
size. Ask for it in plain words:

> Make a page for my drawing series *Harbour at low tide*: a short intro, then the six
> drawings one at a time, and let visitors open each one full size.

The assistant places a `gallery` with **carousel** and **lightbox** turned on. Add your images
in the section's fields (or ask the assistant to use ones from your [Library](/media-studio)),
and write a caption for each — it shows under the image and in the full-size view.

To change an existing gallery, open it in the [Pages room](/page-studio) and set
**Variant** to `carousel` and tick **Lightbox**, or ask: "make the gallery a carousel with
a lightbox".

<Tip>
  The carousel works without JavaScript too: it still swipes and scrolls, and each image
  links to its full-size file. A [static export](/static-export) carries the carousel and
  the full-size images with it.
</Tip>

## How sections stay consistent

- **Tone** — every section accepts a tone (`default`, `muted`, `brand`, `inverted`) that
  picks its surface colour from your brand. Adjacent sections alternate automatically so a
  page never blurs together.
- **Columns** — grids (features, gallery, team, pricing, the `grid` container) take a
  column count; the items reflow responsively on smaller screens.
- **Variant** — some sections offer a named arrangement, such as a `centered` or `split`
  hero, a left/right image on a content section, or a `grid` or `carousel` gallery.
- **Anchors** — every section accepts an `anchor`: a short slug such as `pricing` that makes
  the section reachable as `#pricing` from any link on the page (or `/plans#pricing` from
  another page). Headings inside a `markdown` section get one automatically from their
  text — "## Our team" is reachable as `#our-team`. Ask for it ("add a *See pricing* button
  that jumps to the pricing section") or set it in the section's Appearance fields.
- **Inline formatting** — longer text fields (a subheading, body copy, a bio, quote,
  answer, pricing description, or caption) accept light Markdown: links `[text](url)`,
  `**bold**`, `*italic*`, and `` `code` ``. Just ask for it ("link _Oxford_ to their site")
  and the assistant writes it in. Short labels (headings, button text, names) stay plain.

<Tip>
  Inline formatting is intentionally limited to text marks. For a full block of formatted
  copy — headings, lists, blockquotes — use the `markdown` section instead.
</Tip>

Because the palette is curated and every option is a good one, any page the assistant composes
looks designed — on your brand, every time.
