<Note>
  Saving a draft never touches your live site. A page changes only when you — or a reviewer —
  choose to publish it. The assistant never publishes on its own.
</Note>

## Save draft vs. Publish

Two actions, two very different effects:

- **Save draft** records a forward revision of your work without touching the live site. Your
  in-progress Fernway Botanical Studio spring-sale page is saved, but visitors still see the
  current version.
- **Publish** saves the page as a node, creates a revision, gives you its URL, and takes it
  live. The published page renders chrome-free at its own address.

Publishing a brand-new page also wraps up the conversation, since the work that thread set
out to do is done.

## Where the buttons are

The buttons that move a page sit together at the top right, and they look the same in every
room that edits a page, including **Pages** and **Checks**:

- A **status badge** shows where the page stands: **Draft**, **In review**, **Live**, **Live ·
  draft pending**, or **Archived**. While you have unsaved edits it says so. Hover it for a short
  line on why editing is blocked, when it is.
- **Save draft** saves your edits without going live.
- One main button is the next step for this page: **Publish** on a draft, **Submit for review**
  if you can't publish yourself, **Approve & publish** for a reviewer.
- The arrow beside it opens everything else you're allowed to do, grouped as **Move forward**,
  **Send back** (such as **Reject** or **Restore**) and **Take offline** (**Archive**). Taking a
  page offline asks you to confirm first.

You only see the steps your role allows. So if Fernway's care guide is **In review** and you
aren't a reviewer, there's no Publish button. The badge tells you it's waiting for approval.

If your site has its own review steps (a legal check, say, or translation), they appear in the
same menu. The main button is always the step that takes the page live, or failing that, the
next step forward.

The same state follows you into chat: ask the assistant *"what pages do I have?"* and the page
list shows each page's state for the language you're working in, and every page thread in the
sidebar carries a **Draft** / **In review** / **Live** marker.

## A history you can fall back on

Every publish creates a revision, so your page carries a history. If a change doesn't land
the way you hoped, you have earlier versions to fall back on rather than a single
overwrite-in-place copy.

## Reusable blocks

You can also author **reusable blocks** — sections-only fragments you place by reference
across many pages. A Fernway "Visit the shop" block, for instance, can appear on every care
guide, and updating the block updates it everywhere it's referenced. Blocks follow the same
draft/publish lifecycle as pages, so nothing changes on your live pages until you publish the
block.
