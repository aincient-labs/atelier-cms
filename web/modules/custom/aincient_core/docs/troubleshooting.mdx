---
title: "When something's wrong"
description: "Run a checkup on your Atelier — and let it repair most problems for you"
---

Atelier runs on your own computer, which means when something breaks, the answer is usually
on your machine rather than on a server you can't reach. The manager has a **checkup** that
looks at everything in order and tells you what it found — and can repair most of it for you.

## Run a checkup

**In the desktop app**, go to **System → Troubleshoot** and choose **Run a checkup**. You'll get
a list grouped into *your computer*, *Atelier itself*, and *your website*, with a note under
anything that needs attention. If the problems are ones Atelier can fix, a **Repair what you
can** button appears.

**In the terminal**, the same checkup is one command:

```bash
atelier doctor
```

That only looks — it never changes anything. To repair what it found:

```bash
atelier doctor --fix
```

## What it checks

The checkup works outward-in, because a problem in one layer makes the next one impossible to
judge. If it can't get past a layer, everything below is marked **skipped** rather than failed —
"we couldn't look" and "we looked and it's broken" are different answers, and you'd chase the
wrong thing if they looked the same.

## What a repair does

Repairs go from gentlest to strongest, and stop as soon as your site is well again — so if
clearing a cache was all it took, nothing further runs.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Put back missing setup files">
    If one of Atelier's own files is damaged, it's rewritten. Your old file is kept alongside
    rather than deleted, in case you'd customised it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start Atelier, and wait for it">
    Then it waits until your site genuinely answers, rather than assuming it's ready the moment
    it starts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Clear the caches">
    The single most common cure for a site that's behaving strangely after an update.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Finish any half-applied update">
    If an upgrade stopped partway, this completes it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Repair your uploads folder">
    Occasionally a restore leaves your images owned by the wrong account, which quietly breaks
    new uploads while everything else looks fine.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Run Atelier's self-repair">
    The strongest step. It takes a snapshot of your site first and puts it straight back if the
    repair doesn't help.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  **A repair never deletes anything.** Every step is reversible — the worst case is that it
  takes a few minutes and doesn't help. Removing Atelier, reinstalling it, and restoring a
  backup are *not* part of a repair; if one of those is what you need, the checkup says so and
  leaves the decision to you.
</Note>

## When an action stops partway

Updates, backups, restores and exports show their steps as rows of blocks while they run. If
one stops, the window stays open on the step that failed (marked red), with the error and the
log underneath. Choose **Copy report** to copy what ran, which step failed, how long each step
took and the end of the log. Paste that into your report.

## If it still isn't fixed

<Steps>
  <Step title="Restore a backup">
    If your site itself is damaged rather than just stuck, go to **Backups** and restore the
    most recent one. See [keeping your work safe](/static-export).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Report it">
    Include the checkup's output — from the terminal, `atelier doctor --json` gives a complete
    report you can paste in — and, if an action failed, its **Copy report** text. Add the **Atelier version** from
    [Reports → Status report](/system#which-version-am-i-running) too.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  The checkup is safe to run anytime, and safe to suggest to someone else — on its own it only
  reads. Nothing changes until you ask for a repair.
</Tip>
